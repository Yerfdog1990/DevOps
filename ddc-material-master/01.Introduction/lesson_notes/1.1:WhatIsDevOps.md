# What is DevOps?

## Overview

DevOps is a combination of cultural concepts, guidelines, and various tools that increases an organization's ability to deliver applications and services quickly. By developing and improving various products faster than traditional software development and infrastructure management processes, this speed allows organizations to better serve their customers and compete more effectively in the market.

## How DevOps Works

In DevOps models, development and operations teams no longer work in isolation. Instead, both teams work together as a unified team, with engineers working throughout the entire application lifecycle—from development and testing to deployment and operations. This approach develops unlimited areas of ability beyond traditional role boundaries.

### DevSecOps Integration

In some DevOps models, the quality assurance team and security team work more closely with the development and operations teams throughout the application lifecycle. When security becomes the focus of everyone on the DevOps team, we call this **DevSecOps**.

### Automation and Self-Service

Teams use practices to transform processes that were previously manual and slow into automated ones. They use a set of technologies and tools that allow them to process and develop applications quickly and reliably. These tools also enable engineers to work independently (such as deploying code or providing infrastructure) that would typically require help from other teams, further accelerating the workflow.

## Benefits of DevOps

### Speed
- Take action quickly to innovate for customers more rapidly
- Better adapt to market changes and more effectively drive business results
- DevOps models enable developers and operations teams to achieve results through practices like microservices and continuous delivery
- Teams gain ownership of services and can issue updates to customers more quickly

### Fast Delivery
- Release new models more frequently and faster to innovate and improve products
- The faster you can release new features and fix bugs, the better you can meet customer needs and gain competitive advantage
- Continuous integration and continuous delivery automate the software release process from creation to deployment

### Stability
- Ensure the quality of application updates and infrastructure changes
- Deliver reliably at faster speeds while maintaining a positive end-user experience
- Use practices like continuous integration and continuous delivery to test that changes work and are safe
- Inspection and recording practices provide immediate insight into performance

### Scalability
- Operate and manage infrastructure and development processes at various scales
- Automation and consistency help effectively manage complex or changing systems with reduced risk
- Infrastructure as code helps manage development, testing, and production environments more reproducibly and efficiently

### Improved Collaboration
- Build more productive teams under DevOps' cultural model that values ownership and responsibility
- Developers and operations teams work closely together, sharing responsibility in many cases
- This reduces inefficiencies and saves time by shortening delivery time between teams and writing code with implementation environments in mind

### Security
- Act quickly while maintaining control and compliance practices
- Adopt DevOps models without losing security using automated compliance policies
- Use detailed controls and configuration management techniques
- Infrastructure as code and policy as code help determine and monitor compliance with regulations at various scales

## Why DevOps Matters

Software and the internet have transformed the world and industries—from shopping and entertainment to banking. Software doesn't just support businesses; it's a key component in every sector. Companies interact with customers through software that provides services or online applications on any device. Additionally, software increases productivity by transforming every part of the value chain, such as logistics, communications, and operations.

Just as tangible goods companies changed how they design, build, and deliver products using industrial automation throughout the 20th century, modern companies must change how they create and deliver software.

## How DevOps Models are Implemented

### DevOps Cultural Ideas

Changing to DevOps requires both cultural and conceptual changes. DevOps removes barriers between development teams and operations teams that normally work differently. In some organizations, there may be no separation between development and operations teams—engineers may perform both tasks.

For DevOps, both teams work together to:
- Adjust the developer's productivity rate and operations team reliability to appropriate levels
- Communicate frequently
- Increase efficiency
- Improve the quality of services provided to customers
- Take full ownership of their services, often exceeding traditional role boundaries

The quality assurance team and security team may also be closely integrated with these teams. Regardless of organizational structure, DevOps models include teams that monitor the entire development and infrastructure cycle as part of their responsibilities.

## DevOps Practices Explained

Several key practices help organizations innovate more quickly by streamlining procedures and automating software development and infrastructure management processes. Most practices must be implemented using appropriate tools.

### Frequent, Incremental Updates

One basic practice is frequent, incremental updates that help organizations innovate for customers faster. These updates are typically more incremental than occasional updates under traditional guidelines. Frequent, small updates reduce deployment risk and help teams fix bugs faster because they can identify the specific deployment that caused problems.

### Microservices Architecture

Organizations may need a microservices architecture to make applications more flexible and enable faster innovation. Microservices architecture separates large, complex systems into simple, independent projects. Applications are divided into individual components (services), each with a single purpose or bounded context that works independently of other services and the application as a whole.

This architecture:
- Reduces coordination costs for updating applications
- Enables organizations to act more quickly when each service is assigned to small, agile teams with ownership

However, integrating microservices with increased frequency updates can lead to significantly more deployments, creating operational challenges.

## Core DevOps Practices

### Continuous Integration (CI)

Continuous integration is a software development practice where developers regularly incorporate changes to their code into a central repository, after which it's automatically built and tested. The main goals are to:
- Quickly find and fix bugs
- Improve software quality
- Reduce the time it takes to validate and release new software updates

### Continuous Delivery (CD)

Continuous delivery is a software development practice where code changes are built, tested, and prepared for automatic release to production. This expands upon continuous integration by applying all code changes to testing and/or production environments after the build process. With continuous delivery, developers have built-in effects ready for production that go through standardized testing processes.

### Microservices

Microservice architecture is a design approach for creating a single application as a set of small services. Each service:
- Follows its own processes
- Communicates with other services through well-defined interfaces using lightweight mechanisms (typically HTTP-based APIs)
- Is built according to business capabilities
- Is defined for a single purpose
- Can use different frameworks or programming languages
- Can be deployed as a single service or group of services

### Infrastructure as Code (IaC)

Infrastructure as code is a practice where infrastructure is provisioned and managed using code and software development techniques, such as version control and continuous integration. Cloud API-driven models allow developers and administrators to programmatically interact with infrastructure at various scales instead of manually setting up and configuring resources.

Benefits include:
- Engineers can use code-based tools to interact with infrastructure
- Work with infrastructure the same way as application code
- Infrastructure can be quickly adjusted using standardized formats
- Updated with the latest editors and versions
- Reproduced in reproducible methods

### Configuration Management

Developers and administrators use code to automate operating systems, host configuration, operations work, etc. Using code makes configuration changes:
- Repeatable
- Standardized
- Eliminates the need for manual configuration of operating systems, system applications, or server software

### Policy as Code

Organizations can dynamically monitor and enforce compliance at scale with cloud-coded infrastructure and configurations. This allows them to:
- Track, validate, and automatically reconfigure code-defined infrastructure
- Control changes to resources more easily
- Ensure security measures are properly enforced in a distributed manner (such as data security or PCI-DSS or HIPAA compliance)
- Enable in-house teams to act faster by automatically identifying standardized resources for investigation or automatic compliance

### Monitoring and Logging

Organizations review metrics and logs to see how application and infrastructure performance impacts downstream product user experiences. Collecting, categorizing, and analyzing data and logs generated by applications and infrastructure allows organizations to:
- Understand how changes or updates affect usage
- Gain insights into root causes of unexpected problems or changes
- Monitor services more proactively, especially as services must be available 24/7
- Create notifications or perform immediate analysis of data as applications and infrastructure update with increasing frequency

### Communication and Collaboration

Increased communication and collaboration is one of DevOps' cultural aspects. The use of DevOps tools and automation of software delivery processes creates collaboration by bringing together the workflows and responsibilities of development and operations teams. These teams establish strong cultural norms regarding:
- Information sharing
- Facilitating communication through conversation applications
- Problem or project tracking systems
- Wiki usage

This enables developers, operations departments, and even other teams like marketing or sales to communicate faster, helping every part of the organization adapt to more meaningful targets and projects.
